HOW CRISPR-Cas9
ACTUALLY WORKS
CRISPR-Cas9 is a molecular scissors system borrowed from bacterial immune memory. It lets scientists find a precise location in a genome, cut it, and insert, delete, or replace genetic code — with applications from cancer treatment to drought-resistant crops.

The Editing Process — Step by Step
1
Design Guide RNA
Scientists design a 20-nucleotide guide RNA (gRNA) that matches the exact DNA sequence they want to edit.
gRNA Synthesis
The gRNA is paired with a scaffold RNA that binds to the Cas9 protein. Together they form the search complex.
2
Cas9 Searches DNA
The Cas9 protein binds to the gRNA and slides along the DNA double helix, scanning for a matching sequence.
PAM Sequence
Cas9 looks for a short PAM sequence (NGG) adjacent to the target. Without it, Cas9 won't cut — a built-in safety check.
3
DNA is Cut
When a match is found, Cas9 creates a double-strand break — cutting completely through both strands of the DNA helix.
DSB Created
The break is a blunt cut at a specific position 3 base pairs upstream of the PAM site, on both strands simultaneously.
4
Cell Repair Kicks In
The cell detects the break and activates one of two DNA repair pathways — NHEJ or HDR — each with different outcomes.
NHEJ vs HDR
NHEJ is error-prone — good for gene knockouts. HDR is precise — used when a repair template is provided alongside the CRISPR complex.
5
Edit is Permanent
The repaired DNA is copied into all daughter cells during division. The edit becomes a stable, heritable part of the genome.
Heritable Change
In somatic cells, only the treated cell line is affected. In germline cells, the edit passes to future generations — which is why germline editing remains tightly regulated.

Key Milestones
2012
CRISPR-Cas9 Programmed
Doudna and Charpentier demonstrate programmable DNA cutting. Nobel Prize awarded 2020.
2013
Human Cell Editing
Feng Zhang (Broad) and George Church show CRISPR works in human and mouse cells.
2018
Base Editing
David Liu lab invents base editors — single-letter changes without double-strand breaks.
2019
Prime Editing
Prime editors enable search-and-replace editing — all 12 types of point mutation correctable.
2023
First FDA Approval
Casgevy approved for sickle cell disease. First CRISPR therapy available to patients.
2025
In-Vivo Liver Edits
In-vivo CRISPR therapies show durable efficacy in clinical trials for ATTR and other liver diseases.
