RISE OF
INDUSTRIAL ROBOTS
Factory automation is entering its third wave. After mechanical arms in the 1970s and programmable robots in the 1990s, AI-native robotics is combining vision, language models, and force feedback to create systems that adapt in real time. The number of industrial robots in operation surpassed 4 million units in 2025.

Evolution Timeline
1961
Unimate — First Industrial Robot
GM installs first industrial robot arm at Ewing Township plant. Handles die casting, spot welding. 2,700 lb machine.
1973
ASEA IRB 6 — First Electric
First microcomputer-controlled electric robot. All six axes. Enables precision welding and painting automation.
1997
Collaborative Robots Emerge
Universal Robots founded. KUKA, Fanuc develop lighter arms that can work alongside humans without safety cages.
2012
Amazon Acquires Kiva
$775M acquisition signals mobile warehouse robot shift. 750,000 Amazon Robotics units now run globally.
2020
Vision + AI Integration
Covariant, Vicarious, Dexterous deploy learning-based manipulation. Robots handle novel objects without reprogramming.
2025
LLM-Native Robotics
Physical Intelligence Pi0, Google RT-2 combine vision-language models with robot control. Natural language task programming.

Emerging Trends
VLA
Vision-Language-Action Models
LLMs controlling robot arms via natural language. Google RT-2, Pi0 demonstrate zero-shot task generalization.
DT
Digital Twin Training
Robots trained entirely in simulation, deployed to hardware. NVIDIA Isaac Gym generates millions of training hours daily.
AMR
Autonomous Mobile Robots
Warehouse AMRs replacing conveyors. 1.5M+ units active in logistics. MiR, Locus, Fetch lead the market.
HRC
Human-Robot Collaboration
Cobots now 35% of new sales. Force-sensing and vision mean no safety cage. Universal Robots UR20 lifts 20 kg.
GEN
Generalist Manipulation
Physical Intelligence Pi0 handles laundry, dishes, and packing — diverse unstructured tasks without per-task training.
SWM
Swarm Robotics
Hundreds of small coordinated robots replacing single large machines in warehouses, construction, and agriculture.
